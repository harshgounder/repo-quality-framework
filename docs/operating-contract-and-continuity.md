# Operating contract: preserve the user's objective across handoffs

**Status:** active directive, 2026-11-11. Read after `AGENTS.md` and `RESEARCH_STATE.md`, before selecting a new work batch. If an older checkpoint, summary or example appears to narrow the objective, retain its useful work but follow this contract and the latest user instruction. A later explicit user correction overrides an older interpretation.

## What success actually means

The user wants a broadly usable framework that helps people and AI create, assess, maintain and improve exceptionally high-quality repositories **and the work and real systems they support**. It must investigate what is needed to do the work properly: specialist knowledge and responsibilities, designs, standards, practices, tools, measurements, tests, and eventually evidence-backed, applicable checks and gates. It is intended for many kinds of work and any system, subsystem or implementation—from basic primitives and specialized engines through physical, operational, organizational and user-facing systems. Research should make those capabilities possible, and the framework should eventually help users act and improve.

**A defect/fix corpus, a single rater, static repository scanning, AI evaluation, an individual check, a browser, a settings-dialog example, a matrix, a data store, and a handsome README are each potentially useful pieces—not the mission, a required first domain, or a boundary on scope.** The pre.dev estimator is an initial object of investigation; the inspected snapshot estimates licensing value, so do not present it as a validated general quality rating. Do not assume all useful work is software, all evidence resides in a repository, all quality issues have a prior bug report, or every project needs the same artifacts and controls.

The user rejects shallow generic lists and expects sustained large-scale discovery—thousands of meaningful cases, designs, estimates and other candidates **per relevant class**, not merely tens overall—together with specialist investigation and appropriate levels of depth. Preserve that ambitious direction, not a quota manufactured from duplicate URLs, generated combinations, file counts or unsupported estimates. Show unresolved areas; open-world limits do not authorize arbitrarily stopping discovery. The Atlas is a detailed, expandable discovery source, not the complete system universe or an independently verified authority.

## Independent but connected dimensions

Continuously expand and combine dimensions, rather than picking a few popular systems as the universe. The working model covers, at minimum:

- **Sources and access:** world and regional forges, their instances and communities; social, discussion, Q&A, forums, mailing lists, archives, standards bodies, practitioners, designers, industry, public and private research, labs, benchmark and test artifacts, product documentation, postmortems and historical source/issue data; language, locale, original versus secondary evidence, access and date.
- **Search and selection:** exact questions, vocabulary, aliases, specialist terminology, translations, filters, time windows, source routes, ranking, pagination, sampling and selection; repeat with new terminology and discovered communities.
- **System and work:** any domain, purpose or use; arbitrary recursive components, interfaces, state, data, algorithms, engines, hardware, dependencies, external systems, user journeys and operations; novel builds, rewrites, compatibility replacements, maintenance, migration, research, physical devices and other forms of work.
- **People and lifecycle:** users, operators, professional responsibilities, decisions, handoffs, artifacts, expertise and affected stakeholders across discovery, design, build, verification, deployment, use, maintenance and retirement. Research role families now; the separately promised user list has not been received and is not a blocker. Preserve it entry-by-entry if later supplied.
- **Quality and evidence:** functional behavior, architecture, cleanliness, lean resource use, reliability, performance, security, safety, usability, accessibility, visual/interaction quality, privacy, maintainability, reproducibility, portability, operational recovery, governance and other discovered properties. Separate source claims, observed measurements, direct test results, expert judgment, unknown, not applicable and inaccessible.
- **Approach and decision:** requirements, source-backed practices, standard and edition, implementation, candidate tests/oracles, raters and their mechanisms, benchmarks and workloads, decision rights, costs, false positives/negatives, exceptions, counterexamples, and evidence of real outcomes.

One search, listed value or sample project does not cover an entire dimension. Track relevant **interactions** between values and make unsearched, inaccessible, unsupported and conflicting areas visible. Expand beyond the starter axes and examples as new evidence warrants. Do not force every mathematically possible cross-product into a fake completed result.

## Execute the research and product work

Keep the broad campaign and substantive investigation moving in parallel where practical; neither perpetual high-level planning nor bulk link harvesting replaces the other. At every resumption select an actual question with deliverables, not simply another audit of the handoff.

1. **Discover and investigate related work:** find repository/system raters, standards, quality models, benchmark families, quality and system design projects, specialist tools and leading industry or research practice. Inspect the actual source, implementation or norm where accessible: purpose and assessment unit, version, inputs, algorithm/rules/aggregation, output, unknown and not-applicable handling, applicability, failure modes, evidence and limitations. Distinguish a valuation estimator, popularity/criticality proxy, security tool and overall assessment. Continue beyond existing lists and their languages/platforms.
2. **Connect the Atlas and roles to real work:** process source passages and actual specialized components, not just headers; trace capabilities, algorithms and engines into responsibilities, interfaces, quality obligations, practices and evidence. The supplied Atlas text was previously read in full, but detailed extraction and crosswalk are not complete, and a full read of the external shared conversation has not been established. Re-open original spans when a concrete gap demands it; do not restart completed reading reflexively.
3. **Gather diverse evidence at scale:** continue finding and recording concrete cases, architectures, standards, tests, benchmarks, tools, roles, practices and estimates from many sources and regions. Compare positive and negative cases; follow issue/incident → diagnosis → proposed and accepted/rejected changes → tests → operation where such evidence exists. This is evidence for the quality framework, not a campaign whose final objective is fixing all bugs.
4. **Investigate subtle quality and efficiency:** move beyond obvious lint/file-presence rules. Study behavior at boundaries and over time, duplication and semantic drift, correctness contracts, uncommon specialist failure modes, human/operational handoffs and appropriate stress or conformance techniques. Investigate bloat, unnecessary complexity, algorithms, dependencies, memory, CPU/GPU, size, latency, throughput, startup, build, energy and more with explicit workload, baseline, environment and behavior/compatibility preservation. The video is one motivating illustration, never a template or scope boundary.
5. **Build useful product-facing work:** turn qualified research into contextual quality outcomes, responsibility maps, assessment and improvement guidance, and candidate checks/gates with an observable rationale. Include product/design and human experience as well as engineering and operations. Validate methods with different work types and counterexamples before presenting results as generally reliable. Gate/score architectures remain design choices, not an excuse to avoid eventually building the user's requested framework.
6. **Keep emerging work current:** continually seek new AI/lab and non-AI research, hundreds of relevant benchmarks and their actual task/oracle/limitations, cutting-edge and obscure domain-specific sources. Date/version what was verified; a literature/index row alone is a lead.

The user has authorized research use of Parallel and their signed-in Brave browser/X as well as other available tools; choose appropriate routes and record access limitations. That authorization does not include posting or contacting people.

Maintain source-specific provenance, meaningful candidate classes and distinct counts for discovered rows, distinct source records, canonical identities, opened primary sources, appraised claims, reproduced findings and validated practices. Track individual benchmark suite/task/dataset/paper variants rather than assuming equal URLs or counts mean equivalent entities. Surveyed routes and classes are open to addition; no single platform, model provider, programming language, locale or repository style is privileged as a scope limit.

## Supervise execution with checkable checkpoints

Each meaningful work batch must leave: **(a)** question, dimensions, selection and known exclusions; **(b)** exact executed operations and access failures, with time, query/filters, version and pagination where known; **(c)** actual source inspection and substantive findings, including opposing or negative evidence; **(d)** artifact(s) and an explicit link from evidence to a concrete framework need, assessment or improvement; **(e)** a checker who examines the underlying material and tests where practical, records scope/results and unresolved failures; **(f)** updated requirements/coverage/state and the next concrete action. Independent review is preferred when available; self-review must not be called independent. Report counts with their units and meaning, not as proof of completion.

Every checkpoint is either open, partial, complete for a named bounded scope, inaccessible, or blocked for a specific reason. Check claims, source/version, realistic applicability and counterexamples. A reviewer should challenge at least one favorable conclusion and verify a sample of underlying sources. A complete narrow checkpoint is not completion of the entire project. If a prior instruction was missed, correct the work and update its trace; do not discard it to make status look clean. If external access, terms or a genuine dependency blocks one route, document it and keep advancing other authorized work. Do not repeatedly wait for the user to supply the pending roles list or ask to resume work already authorized.

**Hard anti-drift tests:** reject a handoff or progress claim that (1) changes the primary mission to a bug database, a single interface example or one domain; (2) presents a plan, row count, linked file or source seed as research performed; (3) conflates a source's claim with a reproduced result, a merged fix, a deployed behavior or a validated gate; (4) labels the project finished because a narrow pilot passed; (5) loses the user's scope or the remaining requirements after compaction; or (6) substitutes another planning document for executing the next independent research step. Preserve factual uncertainty without making the ambition smaller.
